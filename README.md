# Survey-of-Embodied-Agentic-RSI

*Abstract*

Embodied foundation models have expanded the range of instructions, observations, and actions that robots can process. Autonomy in execution, however, does not automatically provide autonomy in improvement. Robots must still diagnose failures, acquire useful experience, select interventions, and determine whether accepted changes improve future performance. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) connects these activities through persistent feedback and inheritance, with the stronger objective of improving the mechanisms that produce subsequent improvements. This survey distinguishes within-episode correction, persistent self-improvement, successive version inheritance, and meta-level improvement. It connects large-model learning, sequential agency, and RSI theory, and compares general agent harnesses with their embodied counterparts. It develops a four-axis framework covering improvement targets, update mechanisms, autonomy over improvement decisions, and evidence conditions. Using this framework, we examine reflection and knowledge revision, executable-program evolution, adaptive experience acquisition, parameter learning, world-model revision, and cross-layer intervention selection. We compare representative systems while retaining their assumptions about feedback, validation, transfer, and cost. We also review benchmarks and associated datasets for manipulation, long-horizon decision making, memory, and world modeling, separating pretraining data, improvement experience, candidate validation, and final evaluation. Existing results support several forms of component-level and system-level improvement, but capability gains, improvement autonomy, and demonstrated recursive gains require different evidence. We identify failure attribution, reliable feedback, physical reset, competence preservation, and independent evaluation of revised improvers as central research priorities.
